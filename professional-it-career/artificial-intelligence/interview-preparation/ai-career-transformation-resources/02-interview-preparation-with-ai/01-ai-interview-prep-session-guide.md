## 2. Readiness Diagnostic

### 2.1 Five-Minute Interview Readiness Diagnostic

Try each task and score it from 1 to 5. Use 1 for tasks you cannot complete yet, 3 for tasks you can complete with help, and 5 for tasks you can complete independently. Use 2 and 4 for performance between those descriptions.

1.  I can introduce myself in 60 seconds without reading.
2.  I can explain one real project, lab, or internship clearly.
3.  I can answer "Why should we hire you?" with evidence.
4.  I can troubleshoot a basic network, Linux, or cloud issue step by step.
5.  I can answer honestly when I do not know something.
6.  I can speak for two minutes without filler words or panic.
7.  I can ask three smart questions to the interviewer.

Record your two lowest scores. Practice those tasks first, then repeat the same diagnostic after a timed mock interview. Use the change in scores to choose your next practice tasks.

## 3. Nepal and Global Interview Stacks

Employers may use different interview stages in Nepal, India, the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) countries, and remote hiring. Use the examples below to prepare and confirm your actual stages with the recruiter.

### 3.1 Nepal Interview Stack

| **Nepal stage** | **What usually happens** | **How to prepare** |
|----|----|----|
| Referral / personal network | Many openings move through seniors, training institutes, LinkedIn, or Facebook groups. | Ready a short intro, updated CV, LinkedIn, and proof of labs/projects. |
| Direct HR call or walk-in | HR checks availability, salary expectations, communication, and attitude. | Practice the 60-second intro, salary range, location flexibility, and role interest. |
| Written / basic technical test | Common for freshers: networking basics, Linux commands, fundamentals, MCQs. | Revise fundamentals, commands, subnetting, OSI, DNS, DHCP, ports, ticketing. |
| First technical interview | Tests fundamentals, attitude, and whether you can explain clearly. | Use the layered answer template and the troubleshooting funnel. |
| Manager / senior round | Tests reliability, learning attitude, and trust with users or clients. | Prepare STAR stories, the unknown-answer framework, and smart questions. |
| Salary & joining discussion | Often quick and direct; freshers may accept too fast. | Know your range; ask about shift, probation, training, bond, benefits, growth. |

### 3.2 Global / Remote Interview Stack

| **Global stage** | **What usually happens** | **How to prepare** |
|----|----|----|
| Online application & ATS | Employers may review the application manually or use software to parse and screen it. A recruiter may review your public profile separately. | Use JD language honestly; keep formatting clean. |
| Recruiter screen | Recruiter checks fit, communication, salary range, notice period. | Use CAR for short answers and ask about the process. |
| Recorded video or voice screen | Some employers ask candidates to record timed answers. Review and scoring depend on the employer and platform. | Practice timed answers; do not read scripts. |
| Technical round | Concepts, scenarios, design trade-offs, troubleshooting, or coding. | Use layered answers and explicit "why" reasoning. |
| Take-home task or lab | Common in developer, DevOps, cloud, security, and remote roles. | Document assumptions, steps, limitations, and trade-offs. |
| Panel / final manager round | Assesses autonomy, communication, ownership, and remote readiness. | Prepare project stories, async examples, and smart questions. |
| Offer & contract model | You may be hired directly, through an employer of record (EOR), or as an independent contractor. | Ask who signs the agreement, how compensation is quoted, which benefits apply, and what hours the team expects. |

### 3.3 Nepal vs. Global: The Honest Comparison

| **Dimension** | **Typical Nepal market** | **Typical global/remote market** |
|----|----|----|
| First contact | Referral, walk-in, direct HR call, institute network. | Online application, recruiter search, LinkedIn, ATS, talent platforms. |
| Screening style | Human call; basic fit, attitude, certification, availability. | Automated screen, structured recruiter call, written communication, proof of work. |
| Interview speed | Often faster, sometimes informal. | Often longer, asynchronous, multiple rounds across time zones. |
| What is weighted | Fundamentals, certificate, attitude, willingness to learn. | Autonomy, written clarity, projects, GitHub/labs, remote maturity. |
| Common mistake | Speaking too casually, weak fundamentals, no project proof. | Generic answers, weak written English, unclear time zone, no remote readiness. |
| Nepali student edge | Low-cost home labs; strong English-content access; community support. | Time-zone overlap depends on team hours and season. A documented portfolio helps show what you can do. |

## 4. Background Research with AI

Before you prepare answers, review the company's business, the role, public information about the interviewer, and likely challenges for the team.

### 4.1 What to Research

- **Company:** business model, products, services, customers, recent news, leadership, culture.
- **Role:** required skills, daily responsibilities, growth path, and hidden expectations.
- **Interviewer:** LinkedIn profile, team, technical background, public posts, likely priorities.
- **Industry:** competitors, current trends, compliance pressure, common operational problems.

### 4.2 Prompt: Company Research

Paste the company name and public job description. Use a tool with web access for current research, or provide official sources yourself.

```text
You are a senior career coach with 15 years of experience preparing
candidates for IT interviews across Nepal, India, the GCC, and global
remote markets. Research [COMPANY NAME] and prepare me for [ROLE]. Give me:
1. Company snapshot: business model, products, scale, and recent news.
2. Three strengths the company is known for.
3. Two challenges or risks they likely face.
4. Three industry trends that affect this company.
5. Five talking points that show I have done my homework.
6. Three thoughtful questions I can ask at the end.
Use public sources and include links and dates for current claims. If you cannot browse, use
only the supplied sources and flag what remains unverified. Return a checklist.
```

### 4.3 Prompt: Decode the Job Description

```text
You are an experienced technical recruiter for [ROLE TYPE]. Below is a job
description. Do the following:
1. Identify up to five required skills and rank them by prominence in the job description.
2. Extract good-to-have skills and tools.
3. Generate 10 practice questions based on this job description.
4. List possible unstated expectations and label them as assumptions to confirm.
5. Give me a 7-day preparation plan with daily focus areas.
Output as a markdown table where useful. Be specific, not generic.
JOB DESCRIPTION:
"""
[PASTE FULL JD HERE]
"""
```

**Trainer tip**

Ask the AI to extract requirements, generate practice questions, simulate a round, and explain its feedback.

## 5. Experience Without a Job

### 5.1 What Counts as Evidence

|----|----|

| Lab practice | Packet Tracer, GNS3, EVE-NG, a Linux VM, a Windows Server lab, a cloud free-tier lab. |
| Security practice | TryHackMe, CTF notes, a phishing-awareness project, log analysis, a basic SIEM lab. |
| Documentation | Network diagram, troubleshooting checklist, installation guide, command notes, runbook. |
| Volunteer work | College IT support, community training, cyber awareness, basic IT setup for an organization. |
| GitHub / portfolio | Code, README, screenshots, architecture diagram, project notes, issue log. |

### 5.2 Project Explanation Template

Create an Interview Evidence Bank with one entry per project. Fill in this template for each entry, then use the entries to prepare the [eight stories](#66-the-8-stories-students-should-prepare). Share only artifacts you have permission to disclose.

- **Project name:** what did you build or practice?
- **Problem solved:** what issue or learning goal did it address?
- **Tools used:** which OS, language, platform, protocol, or cloud service?
- **Your exact contribution:** what did you personally configure, code, test, or document?
- **Challenge:** what broke or confused you?
- **Fix:** how did you troubleshoot it?
- **Result:** what worked in the end? Add a screenshot, GitHub link, diagram, or notes.
- **Learning:** what would you improve next time?

### 5.3 Example: Fresher Network Lab Story

**Sample answer**

"In my Packet Tracer lab, I designed a small office network with two VLANs for Admin and Students, and configured inter-VLAN routing, DHCP, and basic access control. My first issue was that clients in VLAN 20 were not receiving IP addresses. I checked VLAN assignment, trunk status, the DHCP pool, and the gateway configuration, then found the DHCP relay address was missing from VLAN 20's gateway interface. The DHCP server was on a different subnet. After adding the relay address, I checked that clients received valid leases. I then tested that the access rules allowed the intended services and blocked the rest. This taught me that troubleshooting should follow the layer and the evidence, not guesswork."

## 6. Behavioral and HR Rounds: Frameworks and Examples

Behavioral interviews evaluate your professional skills, such as problem-solving, communication, and critical thinking, by focusing on your past actions. Interviewers seek concrete evidence of ownership and results, rather than your personal stories. Research ranks structured interviews among the stronger ways to assess job candidates. How well they predict performance depends on the design and role. [Selection research](https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0000994) Using structured frameworks helps you present your experience clearly and prevents rambling.

### 6.1 Four Frameworks You Need

| **Framework** | **Meaning** | **Best for** | **Trigger question** |
|----|----|----|----|
| STAR | Situation, Task, Action, Result | General behavioral questions | Tell me about a time you led, solved, or achieved something. |
| SOAR | Situation, Obstacle, Action, Result | Failure, weakness, conflict | Tell me about a time you failed or faced a challenge. |
| CAR | Challenge or Context, Action, Result | Short HR or AI-screen answers | Give a quick example in 30-45 seconds. |
| PREP | Point, Reason, Example, Point | Opinion and culture questions | What is your work style? How do you handle disagreement? |

### 6.2 STAR Example

**Question:** Tell me about a time you solved a technical problem.

**STAR model answer**

Situation: During my college network lab, our group could not get users from one VLAN to reach the server VLAN.

Task: I was responsible for checking the VLAN and routing configuration.

Action: I verified VLAN membership, trunk ports, gateway IPs, and routing. I found the trunk allowed only one VLAN, so the second VLAN’s traffic was blocked. I updated the allowed VLAN list, tested ping and application access, and documented the steps.

Result: The lab worked, and our group reused the checklist in the next practical session.

### 6.3 SOAR Example

**Question:** Tell me about a time you made a mistake.

**SOAR model answer**

Situation: In an early Linux lab, I edited a service configuration file without taking a backup.

Obstacle: The service failed to start, and I did not immediately know which line caused it.

Action: I checked the service status, reviewed the current boot's service logs with `journalctl -b -u <unit>`, using the actual systemd unit name, compared the file with the documentation, and corrected the syntax. After that, I kept backups before editing config files.

Result: The service started successfully, and I adopted a safer change management process: backup, edit, test, verify, document.

### 6.4 CAR Example

**Question:** How do you handle pressure?

**CAR model answer**

Context: During exam week, I had to finish a Python project and prepare for a networking practical.

Action: I broke the work into smaller tasks, fixed the most urgent errors first, and asked a teammate to review the final output.

Result: We submitted on time, and I learned that pressure becomes manageable when I prioritize, communicate early, and avoid last-minute guessing.

### 6.5 PREP Example

**Question:** Do you prefer speed work or documentation?

**PREP model answer**

Point: I prefer speed with minimum useful documentation, not speed without documentation.

Reason: In IT support and engineering, undocumented fixes create repeated problems for the next engineer.

Example: In my Linux lab, I documented the exact service restart and log-checking steps, so I could repeat the fix quickly later.

Point: So I solve quickly, but always leave enough notes for the team and future troubleshooting.

These examples are just a starting point. Practicing more questions with these frameworks will help keep your answers structured, clear, and relevant to the interview questions.

### 6.6 The 8 stories students should prepare

Prepare eight versatile stories from your own life. Almost every **behavioral question** maps to one of these. Once they are ready, you recombine and reuse them, telling each in CAR (short) or STAR (full) form on demand.

- Leadership or initiative without authority.

> For example: this is a story about a student who took responsibility even when they were not the official leader.
>
> A student noticed that the group project was delayed, so they created a task list, divided work, followed up with members, and helped submit the project on time.
>
> Use for questions like:
>
> “Tell me about a time you showed leadership.”
>
> “Have you ever taken initiative?”
>
> “Why should we hire you?”

Create a story in the same way for the remaining questions based on your experience.

- Conflict with a peer, manager, or stakeholder.
- A failure or mistake you owned and corrected.
- A significant success with measurable impact.
- Teamwork or cross-functional collaboration.
- A tight deadline or high-pressure situation.
- Ambiguity, missing information, or unclear goals.
- Learning a new skill or technology fast.

## 7. Technical Foundations and Troubleshooting

Technical IT interviews often test fundamentals, but strong candidates do more than define terms. They explain how something works, give a real example, troubleshoot the failure, mention trade-offs, and connect it to the job.

| **Domain** | **Must-know interview areas** | **Common question** |
|----|----|----|
| Networking | OSI / TCP-IP, IP addressing, subnetting, DNS, DHCP, NAT, routing, VLAN, VPN, ports. | What happens when you type a URL and press Enter? |
| Systems | Windows / Linux basics, users & groups, services, logs, permissions, patching. | How do you check why a Linux service will not start? |
| Cloud | IaaS / PaaS / SaaS, VMs, storage, IAM, regions and availability zones, security groups, firewalls. | In AWS VPC, how do a [stateful security group](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/vpc/latest/userguide/vpc-security-groups.html) and a [stateless subnet network ACL](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/vpc/latest/userguide/vpc-network-acls.html) differ? |
| Cybersecurity | CIA triad, MFA, least privilege, phishing, vulnerability vs threat, patching, logs. | Explain the CIA triad with a real example. |
| DevOps basics | Git, CI/CD concept, containers, monitoring, automation. | Walk me through what a CI/CD pipeline does. |
| Database basics | Relational and nonrelational data models, queries, indexing, access control, and backup and restore. | What query, consistency, or access patterns would guide your choice of a database? |
| ITSM / Support | Ticket lifecycle, SLA, escalation, incident vs request, documentation. | Incident vs service request, what is the difference? |
| Scripting | Basic Python, Bash, or PowerShell. Read and explain a 20-line script. | What does this script do? |

### 7.1 The Layered Answer Template

Whatever technical question is asked, structure your answer in six layers. This works for freshers and seniors alike, the senior simply goes deeper at each layer.

| **Layer** | **What to say** | **Example for "What is DNS?"** |
|----|----|----|
| 1\. Define | One simple definition. | DNS lets clients look up records for names, including IP addresses. |
| 2\. How it works | Core process in 2-3 sentences. | On a cache miss, the client asks a recursive resolver. The resolver may use cached records or delegations. With no useful cache, it can follow referrals from root and top-level domain servers to an authoritative server. |
| 3\. Real example | Tie it to something you have used. | When I open a website, my system queries the configured DNS resolver. |
| 4\. Troubleshoot | One thing that can go wrong. | Wrong resolver, stale cache, missing record, or firewall blocking port 53. |
| 5\. Trade-off | One limitation or design choice. | Caching reduces lookup time. Resolvers can keep serving a previous answer until its time to live (TTL) expires. |
| 6\. Job relevance | Why it matters for the role. | Test name resolution separately from network reachability and application access. |

### 7.2 The Universal Troubleshooting Funnel

For any scenario question, "the user cannot reach X", "the service is slow", "the pipeline failed", interviewers want a structured funnel, not a guess. Run this every time.

1.  Clarify the scope: one user or many, one site or all, wired or wireless, internal or internet.
2.  Check the obvious: power, cable, Wi-Fi, login, account status, service status, recent change.
3.  Isolate the layer: physical, IP, DNS, application, authentication, permission, security policy.
4.  Gather evidence: commands, logs, packet capture, monitoring, screenshots, error messages.
5.  Form a hypothesis. Start with a read-only test. If a change is necessary, follow approval and rollback procedures.
6.  Fix and verify: confirm from the user side and the system side.
7.  Document and prevent: update the ticket, runbook, monitoring, and knowledge base.

**What interviewers actually score**

Show your reasoning, trade-offs, and evidence. For example, explain what made pandas a better fit than Bash for the data manipulation you performed.

## 9. The Master AI Prompt Library

Copy one prompt and fill in its placeholders with information you can share. Treat any score as practice feedback on that answer. Check technical corrections against official sources, then answer again in your own words.

### 9.1 RCTFT Prompt Framework

| **Letter** | **Element** | **Example** |
|----|----|----|
| R | Role | Act as a senior network engineer with 10 years at an MSP. |
| C | Context | I am a fresh graduate targeting IT Support roles in Kathmandu. |
| T | Task | Ask me 5 troubleshooting scenarios, one at a time. |
| F | Format | For each: question, my answer, score, what was missing, model answer. |
| T | Tone | Be direct and critical. Do not flatter. Treat me as a serious candidate. |

### 9.2 Prompt 1, Master Interview Coach

```text
You are my strict but supportive AI Interview Coach for IT roles. Help me
prepare for real interviews in [select one based on your role: Nepal / India / global remote] markets.
CANDIDATE PROFILE
- Target role: [IT Support / Network / Cloud / DevOps / SOC / Database / Developer]
- Experience level: [fresh graduate / entry-level / mid-level]
- Current skills: [list honest skills]
- Projects, labs, real work: [real evidence only, or a CV with personal and confidential details removed]
- Weak areas: [list honestly]
- Interview round: [HR / behavioral / technical / scenario / final manager]
- Job description: [paste JD if available]
YOUR TASKS
1. Extract the top skills and topics from the role or JD.
2. Ask me one question at a time. Wait for my answer.
3. After my reply, score correctness, clarity, structure, role relevance,
   and technical depth from 1 to 10. Give a short reason for each score.
4. Flag incorrect, vague, exaggerated, or unsupported claims.
5. Ask two follow-ups, one at a time, and wait after each.
6. Then revise my answer using only my real background.
7. After five main questions, report strengths, gaps, and a 7-day plan.
RULES
- Do not invent or assume experience I did not state.
- If my answer is wrong, say exactly what is wrong and how to fix it.
- For scenarios, force me to clarify scope, gather evidence, isolate layers,
test hypotheses, propose prevention.
- Keep HR answers 60-90s; technical scenarios 2-4 minutes.
Start by analyzing my role, then ask the first question.
```

```text
4. CORRECTED VERSION: rewrite my answer as a strong 150-word model response.
Use ONLY the facts and experience I actually gave you, invent nothing.
If my answer was missing something important, mark it clearly as
[ADD YOUR REAL EXAMPLE HERE] instead of making it up.
5. FOLLOW-UP: 2 questions a real interviewer would ask me next.
Rules: Be direct and critical, no flattery. If a claim is factually wrong, correct it and
```

### 9.5 Prompt 4, STAR Story Critic

```text
You are a structured-interview coach who has trained candidates for global IT
roles. Evaluate my behavioral answer against the STAR framework.
Question: [PASTE THE INTERVIEW QUESTION]
My answer:
"""
[PASTE YOUR DRAFT ANSWER]
"""
1. Identify Situation, Task, Action, Result in my answer. Quote my words.
2. Score each component 1-5 with justification.
3. List the top 3 weaknesses.
4. Rewrite my answer as a model 150-word response that fixes weaknesses but
stays factually consistent. Invent nothing.
5. Suggest 2 follow-up questions a real interviewer would ask next.
Tone: direct, professional, no flattery.
```

### 9.6 Prompt 5, Networking / Cloud / Security Scenario Drill

```text
Act as a senior [network / cloud / DevOps / SOC] engineer interviewing me for
a [LEVEL] role. Conduct a five-question drill.
Rules:
- Ask one question at a time. I can answer aloud, then type or transcribe my answer.
  Wait for my reply before moving on.
- Mix concept questions with scenarios.
- After each answer give: score 1-5, what I got right, what I missed, ideal
answer in 3-4 sentences.
- Difficulty ramps up; by Q4 and Q5 I should be working hard.
- After Q5, give a final report card: 3 strengths, 3 weaknesses, 3-day plan.
Begin with Question 1 now. No preamble.
```

### 9.7 Prompt 6, The Devil’s Advocate

```text
You are a skeptical principal engineer reviewing a junior colleague’s answer
or design. Your job is to find every weakness.
My answer or design:
"""
[paste your answer or design]
"""
1. List every assumption I make but did not state explicitly.
2. List every failure mode I did not address: security, scalability, cost,
reliability, vendor lock-in, on-call burden.
3. Ask 3 follow-up questions a real interviewer would use to expose weak understanding.
4. Score the answer or design from 1 to 10 against the role's requirements.
   Explain the score in two sentences based on the supplied evidence.
Be ruthless but professional. Assume I want to improve, not be coddled.
```

### 9.8 Prompt 7, Full Panel Mock Interview

```text
ROLE
You are a panel of three interviewers:
- Interviewer A: hiring manager, behavioral and cultural fit.
- Interviewer B: principal engineer, technical depth in [PRIMARY DOMAIN].
- Interviewer C: peer engineer, collaboration and communication.

CONTEXT
Candidate is applying for [ROLE TITLE] at [COMPANY TYPE].
Experience level: [0-2 / 2-5 / 5+ years].
Background or CV:
"""
[paste your background, key skills, one project story, or a CV with personal and
confidential details removed]
"""

TASK
Run a five-question mock interview.
- Q1: Tell me about yourself.
- Q2: A technical concept.
- Q3: A troubleshooting scenario.
- Q4: A behavioral STAR question.
- Q5: Why this role or employer?

RULES
Ask one question at a time and wait for my answer. Give a short panel note
after each answer and hold the scores until the final report.

FINAL REPORT
1. Per-question scores from 1 to 10 with one-line justification.
2. Three strengths.
3. Three improvement areas with one drill each.
4. Give a readiness assessment based on this mock interview and identify the highest-priority improvement.
5. A 7-day study plan. Begin now with Question 1.
```

### 9.9 Prompt 8, Unknown-Question Handler

```text
Train me to answer interview questions when I do not know the answer.
Step 1: Teach me a 4-line professional structure that shows honesty,
reasoning, and willingness to learn, with no bluffing.
Step 2: Ask five challenging questions, one at a time. Wait for my answer.
Step 3: After each attempt, score me on honesty, structure, and how I framed
the next step to find the answer.
Step 4: Give me a model answer for each.
Target role: [ROLE]. Level: [LEVEL].
```

### 9.10 Prompt 9, Communication & Filler-Word Coach

```text
You are a communication coach. I will paste my recorded answer transcript.
TRANSCRIPT:
"""
[paste transcript]

1. Count the filler words visible in the transcript. Note that automated captions
   may omit fillers or mishear words.
2. Identify any sentence over 25 words and rewrite it shorter.
3. Flag vague phrases and replace them.
```

## 10. The Mock Interview Lab

Form groups of three for a timed mock interview. Rotate after each round so everyone practices each role. If you practice alone, record one answer and score it using the same rubric before reviewing AI feedback.

| **Role** | **Responsibility** | **Focus** |
|----|----|----|
| Candidate | Answer as if it were a real interview. Speak aloud; do not read. | 60-90 seconds for HR, 2-4 minutes for technical scenarios. |
| Interviewer | Ask one question at a time and press for follow-ups. | Stay neutral. Ask "why?" twice. |
| Evaluator | Score with the rubric and give feedback at the end. | Quote specific words. Compare to the rubric, not personal taste. |

### 10.1 Scoring Rubric

Score each criterion from 1 to 5 and save the scores with a short example from the answer. Choose one weak criterion to practice and rescore it on the next attempt.

| **Criterion** | **1-2: Weak** | **3: Average** | **4-5: Strong** |
|----|----|----|----|
| Correctness | Wrong or misleading | Mostly right, small gaps | Accurate, precise, verifiable |
| Structure | Rambling | Some sequence | Clear framework start to finish |
| Technical depth | Only keywords | Basic explanation | Trade-offs and troubleshooting included |
| Role relevance | Generic | Some link to the JD | Strong alignment to role and business need |
| Communication | Low clarity | Understandable | Calm, confident, well-paced |
| Authenticity | Exaggerated | Vague evidence | Honest, specific, credible |

### 10.2 Feedback Language for Evaluators

- Strength: "Your strongest part was when you…"
- **Gap:** "The answer became weak when you said…"
- **Technical correction:** "One technical point to verify is…"
- **Communication:** "Say this more clearly by…"
- **Next task:** "Before the next mock, practise…"

## 11. Global and AI-Screening Track

This section prepares Nepali candidates for formats they may not see in local hiring: one-way screens, asynchronous updates, remote team communication, and live coding or take-home tasks.

### 11.1 One-Way AI Video / Voice Screen

Some employers use recorded interviews. Follow the employer's instructions for the equipment check, preparation window, recording limit, and retakes.

**How recorded answers are reviewed**

Some employers review recordings manually. Others use automated assessments or combine them with human review. For example, Hirevue says its AI-scored video assessments evaluate what the candidate says in the transcript. Check the instructions for your interview. [Hirevue assessment methods](https://www.hirevue.com/platform/our-science)

- Use the prep window to structure with CAR or STAR before you speak.
- Use a specific example from your own work for behavioral questions that ask for one.
- Leave a few seconds before the time limit. Check the platform's timer and recording rules.
- Record in a quiet, well-lit place; test mic, camera, and internet first.
- Practice with AI beforehand. During an interview, follow the employer's rules for AI and disclose any use they permit.

### 11.2 Communicating Across Cultures and Time Zones

Global remote teams run on asynchronous, written communication: Slack, pull-request comments, tickets, and short recorded updates. Your written English and clarity of structure matter as much as live speaking, and that rewards preparation over charisma.

- **Clarity over accent.** No one expects a neutral accent; they expect to understand you. Slow down, structure your sentences, and pause between points.
- **Write like an engineer.** Short sentences, clear asks, no walls of text: what happened, what you tried, what you need.
- **Name the time zone, and do the math.** Nepal is UTC+5:45. A 9:00 AM New York call is 6:45 PM in Nepal during EDT and 7:45 PM during EST. A 10:00 AM London call is 2:45 PM in Nepal during BST and 3:45 PM during GMT. Include the date and both time zones when offering two slots.
- **Send a follow-up note.** Within 24 hours: thank them, mention one specific thing you discussed, restate your interest. Follow the recruiter’s stated contact process.

## 12. Bonus Point: Salary and Offer Conversation

Clarify the role, compensation, and terms before you decide. Ask for a written offer and enough time to review it.

### 12.1 Before the Call

- Research the market range for the role and location. Compare offers in the same currency and pay period.
- Define three numbers: your ideal, your acceptable, and your walk-away.
- Prepare a one-line justification: skills, certifications, projects, market data.
- Clarify shift, probation, bond, benefits, training, remote policy, and review cycle.
- For global roles, clarify contractor vs EOR, gross vs net, taxes, benefits, and working hours.

### Prompt 10, Salary Negotiation Rehearsal

```text
You are a senior career coach who has guided engineers through salary
negotiations in the [Nepal / India / global remote] market. Role-play the HR
partner extending an offer to me.
MY SITUATION
- Target role: [ROLE]
- Current total compensation, if relevant: [AMOUNT or not applicable]
- Competing offers: [DETAILS or none]
- Target compensation and currency: [AMOUNT AND CURRENCY]
- Geographic preference: [LOCATION]
Run a four-stage negotiation. Start with the offer and wait for my reply at each stage.

Turn 2: I push back. You react realistically.
```

## 14. Ethics, FAQs and Common Mistakes

### 14.1 Honesty Audit: After every AI-assisted prep session, answer these to yourself.

1.  Did I actually understand each answer, or did I just memorize it?
2.  Can I defend this answer if asked "why?" three times in a row?
3.  Did I fact-check every technical claim the AI made?
4.  Am I about to claim AI-suggested experience I do not actually have?
5.  Could I reproduce this answer without AI tomorrow morning?

**What never to do**

Use AI in an interview or assessment only when the employer expressly allows it, and follow the disclosure rules. Never paste confidential company data into public AI tools. Never claim a project, tool, or skill you have only seen in a video and cannot defend technically, interviewers find the gap inside 90 seconds.

### 14.2 Common Mistakes

1.  Reading answers like a script instead of speaking naturally.
2.  Using "we" for everything and hiding your personal contribution.
3.  Giving definitions without examples or troubleshooting context.
4.  Overclaiming tools or technologies seen only in YouTube videos.
5.  Ignoring Nepal-market realities: referral calls, direct HR calls, walk-ins, joining-date talks.
6.  Ignoring global-market realities: ATS, AI screens, written communication, time zones, remote autonomy.
7.  Preparing only HR answers and ignoring technical fundamentals.
8.  Preparing only technical answers and ignoring communication, ownership, and salary discussion.

### 14.3 Frequently Asked Questions

| **Question** | **Answer** |
|----|----|
| How long should my answers be? | About 60 seconds for HR, 30-45 seconds for quick CAR answers, and 2-4 minutes for technical scenarios. |
| Should I tailor my resume for each role? | Yes. Match the role honestly with relevant keywords, evidence, projects, and achievements. |
| Is it okay to say "I do not know"? | Yes. A structured, honest answer beats a confident wrong one. Use Prompt 8 to practice it. |
| How do I handle a resume gap? | Do not hide it. Give a real reason, what you learned, and how you stayed sharp. |
| What if AI gives wrong technical info? | Verify commands, product behavior, and vendor specifics against official documentation. |
| What if I freeze in the interview? | Pause, repeat the question, clarify scope, and use the relevant framework. Practiced students recover; unpractised ones panic. |
