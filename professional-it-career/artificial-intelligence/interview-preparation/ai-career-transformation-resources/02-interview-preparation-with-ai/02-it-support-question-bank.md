## Section A, HR & Behavioral Questions

These apply to every IT track, not just support. Use them as practice scripts, not lines to memorize, personalize each with your real projects, labs, internships, volunteer work, or customer-service experience. Where a question needs a story, structure it with STAR (taught in the session guide). Try each question aloud before reading the sample answer. Replace sample claims with evidence from your own projects or work.

**1. Tell me about yourself.**

**Role fit, not life story.** I am an entry-level IT-support candidate focused on endpoint support, user troubleshooting, and service-desk operations. I have built my foundation in Windows, hardware, networking basics, Microsoft 365 concepts, ticketing workflow, and security hygiene. What I bring is a structured support mindset: I listen, confirm the issue, check the basics, document my steps, and escalate with evidence when needed. I am looking for a role where I can support users, learn from senior engineers, and grow into stronger Tier 2 or systems-support work.

**2. Why should we hire you as a fresher?**

**Confidence without arrogance.** Because I understand the junior role clearly. I will not pretend to know everything, but I will follow process, learn quickly, document accurately, communicate respectfully, and ask for help before risking production systems. I have prepared through hands-on practice and I am ready to handle basic tickets while growing into more complex work.

**3. Why do you want to work in IT support?**

**Service attitude + growth path.** IT support is the best place to build real operational discipline because every issue connects users, systems, applications, networks, and business impact. I enjoy solving practical problems and helping people continue their work. I also see support as the foundation for growth into systems, networking, security, or cloud.

**4. What is your biggest weakness?**

**Real but controlled.** My current weakness is limited exposure to large enterprise environments compared with experienced engineers. I am addressing it by practising Windows support, networking basics, ticket documentation, and lab scenarios. In a job, I would close this gap by using the knowledge base, observing senior engineers, and documenting what I learn so I improve every week.

**5. Describe a time you handled a frustrated user or customer.**

**Empathy + control.** I let the user explain without interrupting, then acknowledge the impact, "I understand this is blocking your work", and ask focused questions, set expectations, and give updates. Even if I cannot solve it immediately, I make sure the user knows the case is being handled and documented. People want clarity and progress, not silence.

**6. How do you handle pressure?**

**Discipline under load.** I slow the process down mentally: confirm the impact, prioritize by business urgency, check known issues, perform safe troubleshooting, and communicate updates. I avoid random changes under pressure because they add risk. If the issue is widespread or critical, I escalate early with facts.

**7. What would you do if you do not know the answer?**

**Honesty + method.** I would be transparent and structured: collect the facts, check the knowledge base or official documentation, reproduce the issue if possible, and ask a senior engineer with the details already gathered. I would not guess or make risky changes just to look confident.

**8. Tell me about a time you made a mistake.**

**Safe example, real lesson.** In a lab/support practice I once tried a fix before collecting enough details, and the issue took longer because I had to go back and confirm basics. I learned to document symptoms, ask what changed, and verify scope before applying fixes. Since then I use a checklist approach so I do not skip fundamentals.

**9. How do you communicate with non-technical users?**

**Communication skill.** I avoid jargon and connect the issue to the user’s task. Instead of "DNS resolution failed," I say, "Your computer is connected but cannot translate the website name, I am checking the network name settings." The goal is to keep the user comfortable and informed.

**10. How do you prioritize multiple tickets at once?**

**ITSM thinking.** By business impact and urgency, not by who shouts loudest. A single password reset is usually lower than a department-wide outage. I check SLA, user role, number of people affected, and whether work is fully blocked, and I communicate realistic updates instead of leaving users without status.

**11. Are you comfortable with shift work or onsite support?**

**Adaptability.** Yes. Support often requires shifts, onsite presence, and urgent response. I would confirm the schedule, handover process, escalation contacts, and safety procedures. My priority is reliable support and clear documentation across shifts.

**12. How do you handle confidential or sensitive information?**

**Security mindset.** I follow least privilege and privacy: access only what the ticket needs, avoid viewing personal files, never share passwords, lock screens during work, and document only necessary technical details. If sensitive data is involved, I follow company policy and escalate when required.

**13. What would you do in your first 30 days?**

**Prepared joiner.** Learn the ticketing system, support process, escalation matrix, asset standards, common applications, knowledge base, and communication expectations, while resolving basic tickets correctly, documenting well, and asking good questions to avoid repeated mistakes.

**14. What if a VIP user asks you to bypass policy?**

**Backbone.** I stay respectful but follow policy, explaining the control protects both the organization and the user. If it is urgent, I escalate to the right manager or security team rather than bypassing security myself.

**15. How do you handle feedback?**

**Coachability.** I prefer direct feedback because it helps me improve faster. If a senior engineer corrects my approach, I update my notes and apply the lesson next time. In support, repeated mistakes waste time, so feedback is valuable.

**16. Describe teamwork in IT support.**

**Operations maturity.** Teamwork means sharing context, not just transferring work. If I escalate, I include what I checked, screenshots, logs, impact, and user contact. If I receive a handover, I read the history first and avoid making the user repeat everything.

**17. Why are you looking for your first IT job?**

**Positive framing.** I want to begin my IT-support career somewhere I can apply my foundation, learn enterprise tools, and contribute to user support. I am focused on long-term growth, not just any job.

**18. What are your salary expectations?**

**Don’t anchor too early.** For an entry-level role I am flexible within the company’s range for this position. My priority is the right environment, training, and growth. If you share the budgeted range, I can confirm alignment. (The session guide’s four-turn script covers this in detail.)

**19. How do you handle competing instructions from two seniors?**

**Mature communication.** I clarify priority respectfully. If instructions conflict technically or procedurally, I explain the conflict and ask which direction to follow rather than silently executing contradictory changes.

**20. How do you stay updated in IT?**

**Learning habit.** I follow vendor documentation, practise labs, review common troubleshooting cases, and study foundational certifications like CompTIA A+, Network+, and Microsoft Windows/M365. I keep notes from issues I solve so I can reuse them.

**21. What makes you different from other freshers?**

**Differentiator.** Preparation and process. I am not only reading interview answers; I am practising ticket notes, troubleshooting commands, endpoint scenarios, and communication scripts. I understand support success depends on method, documentation, and user trust.

**22. What is your long-term goal?**

**Growth without sounding temporary.** To become a strong infrastructure, systems, cloud, or security professional. I see support as the foundation because it teaches real user issues, production discipline, and troubleshooting across layers.

**23. How would you explain a delayed resolution to a user?**

**Professional updates.** I give the current status, what has been checked, which team or vendor is involved, and the next expected update, without blaming another team. "We have confirmed this is not limited to your laptop. The network team is reviewing the affected area. I will update you by 3 PM or sooner."

**24. What questions do you have for us?**

**Always have questions ready.** What are the most common tickets for this team? What tools do you use for ticketing, remote support, imaging, and identity? What does success look like in the first 90 days? How does escalation from Tier 1 to Tier 2 work?

## Section B, Technical Questions

Practice one domain at a time. Define the concept, explain why it matters, give an example, and say when you would escalate. The session guide explains the six-layer answer template.

### B1, Networking Fundamentals

**1. Explain IP address, subnet mask, default gateway, and DNS.**

An IP address identifies a network interface for IP communication. The subnet mask or prefix helps the host determine which destinations are local. The default gateway is the next hop when a more specific route is unavailable. DNS returns records for names such as `example.com`. A user can have Wi-Fi connected but still fail to reach the internet if the gateway or DNS is wrong.

**2. What is DHCP, and what does a 169.254 address mean?**

DHCPv4 can provide an address, subnet mask, default gateway, and DNS servers. A Windows device configured for DHCP may assign itself a `169.254.0.0/16` IPv4 link-local address when it cannot obtain a lease. Check connectivity, VLAN, DHCP relay, and server availability. Routers do not forward link-local packets. [IPv4 link-local addresses](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3927)

**3. What is DNS, and how do you troubleshoot it?**

DNS returns records, including addresses, for names. Compare reachability to a known IP with a lookup of the affected name. Use `ipconfig /all` to check the configured DNS servers and `nslookup example.com`, replacing the example name with the affected one. A successful ping does not establish that DNS or the web service works.

**4. What does the ping command test?**

It sends ICMP echo requests and measures the round-trip time for replies. A failed ping can mean the host is down, a firewall is blocking ICMP, a routing problem, or, if pinging by name, a DNS issue. It is useful but not a full application test.

**5. What is the difference between a switch and a router?**

A switch connects devices within one local network using MAC addresses at Layer 2. A router connects different networks and forwards traffic by IP at Layer 3. Offices use switches for local access and routers/firewalls for internet and inter-network traffic.

**6. What does tracert do?**

Windows `tracert` sends probes with increasing hop limits and displays the routers that answer. A timeout or slow response at one hop is not proof of packet loss or delay for forwarded traffic. Check whether later hops and the destination respond. [Windows tracert reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-server/administration/windows-commands/tracert)

**7. What is a VPN, and how do you troubleshoot it?**

A remote-access VPN typically uses an encrypted tunnel over an untrusted network to reach corporate resources. Troubleshooting covers basic internet access, the VPN client status, credentials/MFA, certificate, DNS, split- vs full-tunnel behavior, and whether it affects one user or the whole service.

**8. What is a VLAN?**

A VLAN creates a separate Layer 2 broadcast domain on shared switch infrastructure. Traffic between VLANs needs Layer 3 forwarding. Use routing policy, ACLs, or firewall rules to enforce access between them. A wrong access VLAN can prevent a device from reaching expected resources.

**9. Which ports should a support technician know?**

Common ones: DNS 53 (UDP and TCP), DHCPv4 67/68 (UDP), HTTP 80/TCP, HTTPS 443/TCP for HTTP/1.1 or HTTP/2 and commonly 443/UDP for HTTP/3, RDP 3389, SMB 445, SMTP relay 25, message submission 587, and IMAPS 993. I connect ports to symptoms rather than memorizing them in isolation.

**10. How do you troubleshoot Wi-Fi problems?**

Check the correct SSID, signal strength, password or certificate, airplane mode, adapter driver, IP assignment, authentication, access-point or site scope, and interference, and whether a wired connection works. Enterprise Wi-Fi may involve identity or certificate issues.

### B2, Windows Operating System

**1. What tools do you use for Windows troubleshooting?**

Task Manager, Event Viewer, Device Manager, Services, Disk Management, Reliability Monitor, Windows Update history, Resource Monitor, Command Prompt, and PowerShell. For enterprise issues I also check endpoint management and AD/Entra status if I have access.

**2. How do you troubleshoot a slow computer?**

I check whether it is constant or recent, then review Task Manager for CPU/RAM/disk usage, startup apps, free storage, pending updates, malware symptoms, device health, mapped drives, and app behavior. I avoid reinstalling the OS unless basic checks and safe repairs fail.

**3. What is Event Viewer used for?**

It displays Windows event logs, including system, application, security, and service events. I correlate event time, source, and the user’s symptoms rather than treating every warning as the root cause.

**4. What is Device Manager used for?**

It shows hardware devices and drivers and helps identify missing drivers, disabled devices, conflicts, and errors. For example, if Wi-Fi disappears, I check the network-adapter status and driver here.

**5. How do you troubleshoot a Windows login failure?**

Confirm username/domain, caps lock, network or VPN requirement, account lockout, password expiry, cached credentials, and MFA for cloud logins. Compare another user on the device and the affected user on another approved device when possible. Check AD DS reachability for domain sign-ins or Microsoft Entra sign-in information for cloud access. Shared symptoms help narrow scope but do not prove the cause.

**6. What is Safe Mode?**

Safe Mode starts Windows with a limited set of drivers and services. If the symptom disappears, use that information to narrow the cause.

**7. What do SFC and DISM do?**

For suspected system corruption, run `DISM.exe /Online /Cleanup-Image /RestoreHealth` in an elevated prompt, then run `sfc /scannow`. DISM repairs the Windows component store. SFC checks and repairs protected system files. [Microsoft repair instructions](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/backup-recovery/use-the-system-file-checker-tool-to-repair-missing-or-corrupted-system-files)

**8. What is Group Policy?**

AD DS Group Policy applies user and computer settings such as security rules, mapped drives, printers, and restrictions. Check the device's management method before assuming a setting comes from Group Policy. `gpupdate` refreshes policy. `gpresult /r` shows the resulting set of policies.

**9. What is BitLocker?**

Microsoft drive encryption that protects data if a device is lost or stolen. Before hardware or TPM changes, recovery-key availability matters because BitLocker may prompt for the recovery key.

**10. What is a corrupted user profile, and how do you handle it?**

When Windows user settings or profile data fail to load, symptoms include a temporary profile, missing desktop, or lost app settings. I verify backup/sync status before repairing or recreating the profile.

### B3, Hardware & Endpoint

**1. What is the difference between RAM and storage?**

RAM is temporary working memory for running applications and is cleared at power-off. Nonvolatile storage, such as an SSD or HDD, retains data after power-off. Low RAM causes slowness under load; failing storage causes boot errors, freezes, or data loss.

**2. What are common symptoms of failing storage?**

Slow boot, app freezes, clicking on an HDD, SMART warnings, disk errors in Event Viewer, failed updates, or repeated blue screens. I back up user data before running destructive repairs or replacing the disk.

**3. What is BIOS/UEFI used for?**

BIOS or UEFI firmware initializes hardware and starts the boot process. UEFI can support Secure Boot. Available TPM and virtualization settings depend on the device. In support it matters for boot issues, imaging, BitLocker/TPM, and hardware detection.

**4. What should you do before replacing a user device?**

Confirm backup/data needs, encryption/recovery-key status, required applications, profile or cloud-sync status, asset tag, warranty, and business impact. After the swap, verify login, network, email, printer, VPN, and key apps with the user.

**5. What is ESD and why does it matter?**

Electrostatic discharge can damage components. When handling internal parts, power off, unplug, remove the battery if applicable, and use a grounded wrist strap or antistatic surface, handling components by the edges.

### B4, Microsoft 365, Identity & Access

**1. What is the difference between authentication and authorization?**

Authentication verifies who the user is; authorization determines what they can access. A user may log in successfully but still lack permission to a shared folder or application.

**2. What is MFA?**

Multifactor authentication requires evidence from at least two distinct factor types, such as something you know, have, or are. Some methods combine factors without a password. Follow policy for enrollment, lost devices, and recovery. [Microsoft authentication guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/standards/nist-authentication-basics)

**3. How do you troubleshoot account lockout?**

Verify the user's identity and the account state. Check for recent password changes and devices or apps still using old credentials. Clear the lockout only through the approved process. If it recurs, collect event times and caller information or sign-in evidence, then escalate to the identity team.

**4. What is Active Directory vs Microsoft Entra ID?**

Active Directory Domain Services (AD DS) is the Windows Server directory for users, computers, and groups, and supports domain join and Group Policy. Entra ID is Microsoft’s cloud identity platform for Microsoft 365 and cloud apps. Many organizations use both together.

**5. How do you troubleshoot Outlook not receiving email?**

Check whether one user or many are affected. Test Outlook on the web, review service health, and check mailbox quota, filters, rules, and junk mail. For a missing message, capture the sender, recipient, and time, then request a message trace if authorized. If webmail works, focus next on the desktop app, its connection, authentication, or client settings. Check whether the affected client is classic or new Outlook.

**6. What is OneDrive sync troubleshooting?**

Check sign-in, sync-status icon, storage quota, invalid file names, long paths, permissions, local disk space, and network, and whether the file is cloud-only. Avoid deleting local folders before confirming sync and backup state.

**7. What is least privilege?**

Users should have only the access required for their job. In support, I do not grant admin rights casually, I follow the approval workflow and document access changes.

### B5, Security & ITSM

**1. What are common signs of phishing?**

Urgent language, suspicious sender, mismatched links, unexpected attachments, password requests, payment or gift-card demands, and spoofed login pages. I report per policy and do not click or forward suspicious content.

**2. What is the difference between an incident and a service request?**

An incident is an unplanned interruption or degradation of service, the goal is to restore service fast. A service request is a standard request such as software installation or access, following a defined approval process.

**3. What is an SLA?**

A service-level agreement sets service commitments. For support, those may include response or restoration targets and the hours when the clock runs. Use the team's impact and urgency rules to set priority, and check the applicable target.

**4. What makes a good ticket?**

Record the affected service, user or device, start time with time zone, exact symptom, scope, impact, checks and results, owner, and next update. Attach only evidence that the receiving team is authorized to see. Record the recovery test or handoff before closing.

**5. What is patch management?**

The controlled process of deploying updates to fix vulnerabilities, bugs, and stability issues. Support helps by ensuring devices receive updates and reporting failures.

**6. What is the difference between an incident and a problem?**

An incident is an interruption or reduction in service quality. A problem is a cause or potential cause of one or more incidents. Restore printing for affected users and track the recurring cause in a problem record.

**7. What is change management?**

Controlling modifications to systems to reduce risk. Even simple production changes may require approval, communication, a rollback plan, and a maintenance window.

## Section C, Troubleshooting & Scenario Questions

For each scenario, state your hypothesis, explain the safest next action, and describe how you would verify recovery or hand off the case. Use the [troubleshooting funnel](01-ai-interview-prep-session-guide.md#72-the-universal-troubleshooting-funnel) to structure your answer.

**1. A user can ping 8.8.8.8 but cannot open websites by name.**

**Working hypothesis:** Name resolution is a useful first hypothesis. A reachable public IP alone says nothing about browser access.

**Safe response:** Query the affected name using the configured resolver with `nslookup`. Compare a working device on the same network. If the lookup succeeds, test the browser, proxy, VPN, or application path. [Microsoft nslookup reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-server/administration/windows-commands/nslookup)

**Verify or hand off:** Confirm both a successful lookup and access to the original site. Record which check failed if you escalate.

**2. A user’s device shows a 169.254.x.x IP address.**

**Working hypothesis:** The device may have fallen back to IPv4 link-local addressing after it failed to get a DHCP lease.

**Safe response:** Use `ipconfig /all` and confirm the adapter, VLAN, and scope of impact. Check DHCP relay and service availability through the approved route. Renew the lease when authorized and safe for the session. [Microsoft ipconfig reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-server/administration/windows-commands/ipconfig)

**Verify or hand off:** Check for an address on the expected subnet and test the user's original network task.

**3. Outlook web works, but desktop Outlook fails.**

**Working hypothesis:** Local profile, cache, credential, or add-in issue.

**Safe response:** Check which Outlook client the user has, then check sign-in, service health, updates, and client-specific settings. For classic Outlook, check add-ins and the profile. Confirm where mail and local data are stored before recreating a profile.

**Verify or hand off:** Send and receive an approved test message in the desktop client. Confirm the user can complete the original task.

**4. One user cannot access a shared folder; a teammate can.**

**Working hypothesis:** Permission or group membership.

**Safe response:** Confirm the correct share path and the user's approved access. Check effective share and file-system permissions where authorized. If group membership changed, refresh the sign-in session according to policy.

**Verify or hand off:** Test the requested operation with the user's approved account. Confirm access is limited to what was granted.

**5. All users in one area lose network at once.**

**Working hypothesis:** Switch, access point, VLAN, uplink, or power.

**Safe response:** Confirm scope, collect the location and affected ports, check the local network closet if authorized, and escalate to the network team with evidence.

**Verify or hand off:** Confirm a representative device in the affected area can reach the original service. Keep the incident with its assigned owner until scope and recovery are clear.

**6. A laptop asks for the BitLocker recovery key after a hardware change.**

**Working hypothesis:** TPM/secure-boot change triggered recovery.

**Safe response:** Do not bypass it. Verify identity and the device's recovery key ID, then retrieve the key through the approved process. Record the hardware change without recording the key. Escalate repeated recovery prompts before changing firmware or TPM settings.

**Verify or hand off:** After approved recovery, confirm the user can sign in and BitLocker protection remains enabled. Escalate repeated prompts.

**7. A user asks you to share another employee’s password.**

**Working hypothesis:** The user may need access to a resource assigned to someone else.

**Safe response:** Decline to share credentials. Use the approved access request or delegation process for the resource.

**Verify or hand off:** Confirm the access request reached the responsible owner. Test only the access granted to the requester.

**8. A printer works for everyone except one user.**

**Working hypothesis:** Local driver, queue, default printer, profile, or permission.

**Safe response:** Compare the print-server mapping and the user’s local queue and driver.

**Verify or hand off:** Send a test job from the affected account to the intended queue and confirm it prints correctly.

**9. New software works for an admin but not for a standard user.**

**Working hypothesis:** Permission, install context, missing dependency, or profile setting.

**Safe response:** Capture the exact error and reproduce it with an approved standard account. Identify the missing dependency or access requirement before asking the application owner to change packaging or permissions.

**Verify or hand off:** Retest with a standard account and confirm the fix did not grant extra rights.

**10. VPN works from home Wi-Fi but not from hotel Wi-Fi.**

**Working hypothesis:** Captive portal, blocked ports, DNS, or network restrictions.

**Safe response:** Check whether the network requires a captive portal and follow the approved connection procedure. Compare with an approved alternate connection. Collect the VPN client error and logs if the problem persists.

**Verify or hand off:** Confirm the tunnel connects and an approved internal resource opens.

**11. A user reports repeated account lockouts every few minutes.**

**Working hypothesis:** A device or app may be retrying stale credentials. Unexplained activity can also require security review.

**Safe response:** Check the timing against saved credentials on devices, mapped drives, scheduled tasks, services, and VPN clients. Ask the identity team to correlate repeated failures with the account's sign-in evidence.

**Verify or hand off:** Confirm sign-in works and the lockout does not recur during the normal retry interval.

**12. A user says the computer is "hacked" because pop-ups keep appearing.**

**Working hypothesis:** Browser notifications, an unwanted extension, adware, or a compromise are all possible.

**Safe response:** Check which app displays the pop-ups, browser notification permissions, extensions, and endpoint security alerts. Preserve evidence and escalate through the security process if indicators suggest compromise.

**Verify or hand off:** Confirm the pop-ups no longer occur, or record that security owns the incident before handing it off.

**13. A user reports the laptop is slow only in the afternoon.**

**Working hypothesis:** Scheduled scans/updates, thermal throttling, or network congestion.

**Safe response:** Check Task Manager during the slowdown, scan/update schedules, temperatures, and whether others on the same network are affected.

**Verify or hand off:** Compare the same task during the affected time window after any approved change. Record the measurement and ask the user to confirm.

**14. A shared-mailbox "send as" fails.**

**Working hypothesis:** The user may lack Send As permission, or a recent grant may not yet be effective.

**Safe response:** Confirm the requested permission and test Outlook on the web and desktop as applicable. Full Access allows mailbox access but does not grant Send As. Send on Behalf is a separate permission. [Exchange Online permissions](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/exchange/recipients-in-exchange-online/manage-permissions-for-recipients)

**Verify or hand off:** Use an approved recipient to check that the message sends and shows the intended sender.

**15. A remote user cannot print to an office printer.**

**Working hypothesis:** VPN policy or printer mapping.

**Safe response:** Check whether remote printing is supported, VPN connectivity, printer mapping, driver, and network reachability.

**Verify or hand off:** Confirm an approved test page reaches the intended office printer. If policy disallows remote printing, document the supported alternative.

**16. A clinical label printer prints the wrong format.**

**Working hypothesis:** Wrong printer, driver, label size, or app template.

**Safe response:** Alert the clinical team and follow its downtime process if labels could be unsafe. Test the printer, driver, label size, application template, and mapping with approved test data. Confirm the printed result before returning it to use.

**Verify or hand off:** Ask the clinical owner to confirm that the approved test label matches the required format before returning the printer to service.

**17. A user clicked a phishing link and entered their password.**

**Working hypothesis:** Potential credential compromise.

**Safe response:** Report to security immediately and preserve the message and event times. Authorized responders should contain the account, reset compromised credentials, revoke active sessions, and check sign-ins, MFA methods, and mailbox rules or forwarding. Follow the organization's incident process. [Microsoft 365 compromise response](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/responding-to-a-compromised-email-account)

**Verify or hand off:** Confirm that the security team owns the case and has recorded containment. Restore account access only through the approved incident process.

**18. Multiple users report a business application is down.**

**Working hypothesis:** A shared dependency such as the app, identity service, license service, or network may have failed.

**Safe response:** Confirm scope, check the app/service status and known issues, avoid local fixes, and escalate to the application team with impact details.

**Verify or hand off:** Confirm recovery with representative users and monitoring. Record affected scope, restoration time, and any unresolved symptoms.

## Section D, Phone-Screen & Recruiter Questions

These quick questions decide whether you reach the technical round. Keep answers short, honest, and positive, usually 20-40 seconds each.

**1. Do you have experience?**

I am entering as a fresher/junior candidate, but I have prepared through hands-on labs and structured troubleshooting practice. I am ready for basic tickets and eager to learn enterprise tools quickly.

**2. Are you open to onsite work?**

Yes. Support roles often require desk-side or field work. I am comfortable with onsite troubleshooting, device setup, and professional user interaction.

**3. Can you work shifts or weekends?**

I am open to shift-based support depending on the schedule and handover process. I understand IT operations may need coverage outside standard hours.

**4. When can you join?**

I can join based on the company timeline and am prepared to start quickly after the hiring process.

**5. What roles are you targeting?**

Help Desk, IT Support Technician, Desktop Support, Field Service Technician, EUC Technician, and junior infrastructure/support roles.

**6. What is your expected salary?**

For an entry-level role I am flexible within your range for this position. If you can share the band, I can confirm alignment. My priority is the right environment and growth.

## Section E, Role-Specific Questions

Once you know the exact role, expect a few questions tuned to it. Prepare the ones that match your target, they show you understand the job, not just IT in general.

### E1, Help Desk / Service Desk

**1. How do you decide a ticket’s priority?**

By business impact and urgency, how many users are affected, whether work is fully blocked, the user’s role, and the SLA, not by who contacts me most loudly.

**2. What goes into a good escalation?**

User, device, location, exact error, time it started, impact, troubleshooting already done, logs or screenshots, and the requested next action, never an empty ticket.

### E2, Desktop / EUC Technician

**1. Walk me through imaging and setting up a new laptop.**

Follow the employer's build method: deploy its approved image or provision the existing Windows installation with a tool such as Windows Autopilot. Confirm the expected AD DS or Microsoft Entra join and management enrollment. Verify drivers, applications, updates, encryption, login, network, email, printers, and VPN before handover. [Windows Autopilot overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/autopilot/overview)

**2. How do you handle a hardware swap with encrypted data?**

Confirm backup and the BitLocker recovery key first, document the asset, perform the swap, then verify the user can log in and access their data and key applications.

### E3, Field Service / IT Field Engineer

**1. How do you prepare for an onsite visit?**

Confirm the issue, location, access, contact person, and required parts/tools; bring spares; and plan to capture evidence. I keep the user updated on arrival and resolution.

### E4, Tier 2 Support

**1. How do you isolate an issue Tier 1 could not solve?**

I review what Tier 1 already tried, reproduce the issue, isolate by layer (device, network, identity, application), gather evidence, test the smallest safe change, and document so the fix is repeatable.

### E5, Junior Systems Administrator

**1. Why is change control important even for small changes?**

Because small production changes can have wide impact. Approval, communication, a rollback plan, and a maintenance window reduce the risk of an outage and make recovery faster if something breaks.

### E6, Clinical IT Support

**1. How is clinical IT support different from office support?**

It affects patient-care workflows, downtime can delay appointments, medication, imaging, or documentation. I prioritize safety, privacy, and urgency, follow downtime procedures, and escalate to clinical/application teams quickly.

**2. What must you remember when supporting healthcare systems?**

Protect sensitive health information, access only what is needed, verify identity, avoid exposing patient data, document carefully, and balance technical speed with privacy and patient-care impact.
